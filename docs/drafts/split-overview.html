Asset split + generator/bootstrap separation (#134 v3)

The split, mapped

What each top-level part of the repository would do once the asset split and the #134 separation are both built, which parts may rely on which, and where the code would still repeat itself. Written for deciding whether the cut is in the right place.

Sources: the moved tree (wip/bmove), the draft docs/drafts/separation-v3.yaml, and a DRY analysis measured with diff on 2026-11-02. Figures marked "measured" were counted; judgements of deliberate or accidental are estimates.

Who may rely on whom

An arrow means "may import". Everything points down towards domain/, which imports nothing. The real pipeline never reaches for anything synthetic, so a real deployment can ship without the generator, scenarios/ or the bootstrap. A new mothman check gate (REQ-TEST-384) fails any import across a forbidden edge.

What each part does

PartIn plain language
assets/<asset>/configurationEverything one data asset is: its collections and datasets, calendar, people, the checks (contracts, Soda, Evidently, its own dbt project), the small QA adapter per collection, and a synthetic/ folder that only a synthetic asset has. Two today, quarterly and daily. Configuration, never state.
dbt_shared/configurationThe dbt macros and generic tests both assets use, held once.
domain/shared, ~3,700 linesThe vocabulary everyone agrees on: the asset's own clock and time zone, the hierarchy of agencies and collections, the delivery schedule and its slots, and the pure rules the generator and pipeline both need (which slot a supply fills, how an arrival is classified, a check's warn and fail bands). It reads configuration but imports nothing, opens no database, and holds no supply rows.
qa_tools/real pipelineThe QA pipeline itself: receives and stages a supply, runs dbt, Soda, datacontract-cli and Evidently, files it to a period, applies the promotion rule, records results and decisions in the database. It reads the time through one clock seam and never plays a scripted person.
pipeline/real pipelineTurns recorded QA results into the dashboard's data. Reads recorded results only, never supply rows.
dashboard/real pipelineThe single-file dashboard page, its build and its one publish path. On a real asset its scenario features are hidden.
cli/front doormothman, the one entry point. Synthetic commands (generate, bootstrap, scenarios) appear only where the synthetic parts are installed and the asset has a synthetic/ folder.
scenarios/synthetic, sharedA small package both synthetic parts read: the scripted-decisions file reader and the scenario vocabulary (the TS-numbered planted cases).
generator/syntheticInvents supplies: writes delivery files, their receipts and the scenario map, using each collection's provider. Knows only the domain model and the scenarios, never the pipeline's internals.
bootstrap/synthetic, ~3,500 linesReplays a generated history through the real pipeline from empty: runs the generator, feeds the pipeline one arrival at a time under a simulated clock, and plays the scripted people's decisions through hooks. Refuses any asset not declared synthetic.
synthetic_data_generator/exploratoryThe separate population-scale generator, still unwired, behind mothman population.

How DRY it will be

Mostly good where it matters. Things already held once:

  • dbt macros and generic tests (dbt_shared/).
  • Warn and fail thresholds, read from each tool's own files rather than copied.
  • File checks, defined once with ids derived per dataset.
  • Status words in Python and the page, held equal by shared test cases neither side owns. This copy is justified: the page has no build step and ticketing runs without a browser.
  • Moving the generator's shared functions into domain/ removes the direct sharing: 75 of the 122 names it imports come from there.

Between the two assets, most "repetition" is deliberate. Contracts, checks, adapters and providers share only 10 to 25% of their lines (measured), mostly YAML keys, because they describe different collections for different teams.

The real risks, most important first:

  1. Allowed values in up to five places per collection

    A list such as valid postcodes can sit in the contract, the Soda checks, the dbt schema, the QA adapter and the generator, with no test holding them equal (measured).

    Would fold it: declare each list once and have the others read it, or at least an agreement gate.

  2. Byte-identical files in both assets

    file-checks.yaml (118 lines), the dbt project boilerplate, a 35-line forwarding macro, most of people.yaml and much of data-asset.yaml are identical in both assets (measured). They will drift one asset at a time.

    Would fold it: a shared default with per-asset override, plus an "identical unless overridden" gate. Your answers already rename the dbt projects and drop the forwarding macro (C12, C13).

  3. Near-copies the function move misses

    The status ranking exists four times, one on each side; the Child Protection provider restates the quarterly calendar's quarters; generator/dirty.py hard-codes fault rates sized to Soda thresholds written in comments; one table of expected verdicts is hand-written.

    Would fold it: the status order in domain/, and the older providers reading thresholds through the same reader Linkage Products already uses.

  4. Geocoding checks, about 330 hand-copied lines per new source

    Each geocoded dataset's Soda, dbt and contract files are near-copies (measured). Deliberate: you decided on 28 October that these checks are hand-written.

    Would fold it: only worth it if many sources are coming. The file-checks "derive per dataset" pattern is the template.

  5. Status copies inside the page itself

    Two tables in the dashboard template repeat its own status words and order.

    Would fold it: point them at the one copy the page already has. A small fix.

What to decide

  1. Is the cut right: a shared domain/, a real pipeline that never sees synthetic code, and three synthetic parts that a real deployment leaves out?
  2. Should identical per-asset configuration become a shared default with per-asset override, or stay copied because each asset is its own deployment?
  3. Should allowed-value lists be declared once, or get an agreement gate, or neither for now?
  4. Should the status order and the generator's near-copies join the #134 work, or wait?

After your decisions, the draft goes back to the scoper or on to the architect and the CLI and dashboard UX reviewers before sign-off.
